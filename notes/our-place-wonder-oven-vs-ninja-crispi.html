Wonder Oven sizes vs Ninja Crispi

Standard, Large, and Pro against a glass PowerPod that leaves the counter.

August 15, 2026 · Chicago

Our Place sells a countertop oven that air-fries. Ninja sells a pod that sits on a glass bowl. Retail pages file both under “air fryer” because that is the aisle shoppers search. They are not the same object. One is a box that stays. One is a lid that leaves. August 2026 is a decent month to say that without a sale banner. The fork is not brand loyalty. It is whether you need a cavity with racks, or a vessel you can put in the fridge.

Three Wonder Ovens

The Standard Wonder Oven is about 12 liters, two rack positions, three knobs, six modes: air fry, bake, roast, toast, broil, reheat. Our Place pitches it at two to four people. The Large is about 28 liters, three levels, same six modes, same knob language, aimed at four to six. The Pro keeps the large box (28 to 30 liters depending on the sheet you read) and swaps knobs for a digital panel, adds dehydrate and proof, and uses quartz plus stainless elements. Steam infusion is the Our Place sentence across the line: a little water in the system so the oven is not only a dry box.

The Standard is a compact oven that will take a small chicken and a pair of slices of toast. It is not a Dual Zone. It is not a 28-liter family box. If you toast daily and roast a bird on Sundays, Standard is the size that earns the counter. Large is the size that replaces a second oven in a house that already has a wall oven you do not want to heat in August. Pro is Large with a panel and two extra verbs. Do not buy Pro for the word digital if you liked the knobs. Do not skip Large for Standard because the photograph looked the same.

Interior chemistry is part of the pitch: stainless, ceramic coatings, a PFAS conversation Our Place wants to have and Ninja mostly does not. That is a materials preference, not a cooking mode. If the reason you are here is coating, stay in the Our Place line and pick a size. If the reason you are here is speed and a lid you can store, keep reading.

Crispi is a lid

The Ninja Crispi PowerPod is a heating head. It clamps onto a 4-quart glass container or a 6-cup container. Max Crisp, Air Fry, Bake, Recrisp. The glass is the cookware. You can start frozen, finish crispy, put a lid on, and refrigerate. You can take the small vessel to a desk. You cannot toast six slices. You cannot run two temperatures at once unless you own two pods. A 4-pound chicken is the listing boast; height still matters, and the bird is a squat bird.

Crispi’s virtue is negative space. When the pod is in a cabinet, the counter is a counter. Wonder Oven never does this. Even the Standard is a box you will not put away after the first week, because putting it away is a two-handed, two-minute job you will not do on a Tuesday. If the kitchen is a galley and the toaster is already a grievance, Crispi is the air-fryer that does not add a second grievance. If you want toast and a sheet of cookies, Crispi is a lid pretending to be an oven.

Jobs, not brands

Toast: Wonder Oven, any size. Crispi will recrisp a slice and will not replace a toaster. Pizza: Large or Pro, in the 12-inch class; Standard is a personal pie; Crispi is not a pie. Two foods at once: neither of these; that is Dual Zone. Leftovers in the same vessel they cooked in: Crispi. A tray of vegetables for four: Large or Pro, or a sheet pan in the wall oven. A single salmon portion at lunch: Crispi 6-cup. A proofed loaf: Pro, or the wall oven. The overlap is “make this chicken less pale.” Both can do that. They do not do it as the same furniture.

Preheat culture differs. A Wonder Oven is an oven; it wants a few minutes. Crispi is a small mass of glass and a blast of a pod; it is ready when the fan is ready. Frozen fries in Crispi are the demo. A sheet of roasted broccoli in the Large Wonder Oven is the demo. If your weeknights are frozen bags, the pod wins more evenings. If your weeknights are a tray and a thermometer, the oven wins, and you should also read the sheet-pan note before you add a third heat source.

Counter, noise, wash

Standard Wonder Oven is already a toaster-oven footprint: depth that eats a US counter from wall to edge. Large and Pro are honest about being family boxes. Crispi’s pod is small; the 4-quart glass is a mixing-bowl footprint you can park. Noise is fan noise on both. The oven is a longer, lower note. The pod is a shorter, angrier one. Dishwashers like Crispi glass and do not always like Wonder Oven pans. Hand-washing a 28-liter cavity is a different hobby than rinsing a bowl.

US 120V, August 2026. Prices move; the sizes do not. Standard versus Large is the Our Place decision. Crispi versus any Wonder Oven is the category decision. If you want one object and you toast, take a Wonder Oven in the size that matches the household. If you want one object and you do not toast, and you need the counter back, take Crispi. If you want both jobs, you are shopping two objects, and you should be honest about that before a listing sells you a “system.”

Materials, steam, and what that does not buy

Our Place wants the conversation to be coatings and steam. Ninja wants the conversation to be watts and a chicken in a glass jar. Both conversations are real and neither is a reason to ignore size. Steam in a Wonder Oven is a moisture assist, not a combi-oven. It helps bread and it helps a chicken skin from turning into cardboard. It will not braise. Crispi has no steam story. It has thermal-shock-resistant glass and a warning not to be silly with a cold wet bowl under a screaming pod. Read the glass manual. Then use the vessel as cookware, which is the feature.

Replacement parts tell you how a company expects the object to live. Our Place sells racks, pans, and a cavity that is the product. Ninja sells extra glass, extra lids, a spare pod if you are unlucky. If you break a Wonder Oven element you are in warranty land. If you break a Crispi bowl you can, in principle, have a bowl again without a new head. That is a small ownership fact that listings skip. It is also why Crispi is the one you can take to a rental kitchen in a month you do not own a toaster oven.

Do not buy both on the same day. Use a sheet pan in the wall oven for a week and notice which job you actually missed: toast, a small fast cavity, or a vessel that travels. Then pick Standard, Large, Pro, or a pod. The brand names are the easy part. The counter is the part that stays.
