Air fryers

Oil, spray, and preheat in air fryers

Aerosol gum, dry flour, and when a cold basket is a feature.

August 22, 2026 · Chicago

Air fryers are small convection ovens with a marketing department. Oil still matters. Preheat sometimes matters. Aerosol sprays matter in the wrong direction: they leave lecithin and propellant gum on nonstick until the basket looks like a cloudy film and then a sticky one. August 2026, US kitchens, Dual Zone drawers and glass Crispi vessels included. This is not a recipe. It is the physics that recipes skip.

Aerosol is a coating, not an oil

The grocery-aisle “for air fryers” spray is often still an aerosol. Aerosols that list propellant and emulsifiers bake onto baskets at 390°F in a way a teaspoon of oil does not. Ninja, Our Place, and every other nonstick warrantypage says some version of this. The gum is not theoretical. It is the reason a six-month-old drawer feels tacky after a rinse. Use a pump mister with actual oil, or a bowl and your hands, or a brush. If the bottle hisses, it is a suspect.

Amount: enough to gloss, not enough to puddle. Frozen fries often already carry oil; more oil just smokes. Fresh potatoes, tofu, and breaded chicken want a real film or the flour stays pale and dusty. Dry flour on dry metal is how you get a bland crust and a dirty basket. Toss in a bowl. Do not mist from two feet away and call it coverage. The drawer’s corners will stay dry and the middle will go blotchy.

When a cold basket is a feature

Preheat is useful when the food needs an immediate hard heat: a slice of leftover pizza, a thin cutlet, a handful of wings that should not steam in a rising drawer. Preheat is wasted when the food is a dense frozen brick that will sit for eighteen minutes anyway. In a Dual Zone, preheating both drawers for a bag of mixed vegetables is theater. In a Crispi, the glass starts cooler than a metal drawer; the pod is the heat. Give the pod a minute if the manual says so. Do not mimic a restaurant salamander.

Some foods prefer the cold start. Bacon in a basket will throw fat; starting cold lets the fat render without an opening blast that spatters the heater. Delicate fish can pick up a scorched bottom if the crisper plate was preheated empty to 400°F. Cookies in a toaster-oven-style cavity want the preheat; cookies in a drawer usually should not exist. Match the start to the food, not to a universal “always preheat” sentence copied from a 1970s oven manual.

Crowding is still the main heat

Oil and preheat cannot rescue a drawer stacked three deep. Air fryers brown by moving hot air over surfaces. Surfaces that touch other surfaces steam. Shake, space, or do a second round. The 10-quart Dual Zone forgives crowding more than the 8-quart; it does not repeal the rule. A mister over a crowded pile oils the top and leaves the bottom pale. If you need volume, the sheet-pan note is the rest of the argument.

Liners: perforated parchment is a truce. Solid foil is how you block the air the machine exists to move. Silicone cups are fine for muffins and silly for fries. If the liner sits on the crisper plate and seals the holes, you have built a tray. Own that and use the oven.

Smoke, soap, and the long film

Smoking oil is usually leftover grease on the heater, not the teaspoon you just added. Wipe the drawer after wings. Wash the crisper plate. A 400°F preheat of a dirty machine is an incense practice. If the kitchen smokes, drop the temperature 25 degrees before you add more oil. High-smoke-point oils (avocado, refined peanut, plain canola) are boring and correct. Extra-virgin olive oil is a flavor at 350°F and a smell at 400.

Soap and a non-scratch sponge beat a second coat of spray. Dishwashers shorten nonstick life; they also get used. If you dishwasher, skip the aerosol even more. The film plus high heat plus detergent is how a basket goes from slick to sandy in a season. US water, US detergent, August humidity. Dry the drawer before the next round or you are steaming from the first minute, which makes people add more oil, which makes more gum. The loop is optional. The cold basket, the pump mister, and a little space are the whole technique.

A short working list

Leftover pizza: hot drawer, no extra oil, two to four minutes, watch the cheese. Frozen fries: cold or brief preheat, no extra oil unless the bag is a sad bag, shake once. Fresh hand-cut potatoes: rinse, dry, toss with a spoon of oil and salt, 400°F, do not stack. Chicken wings: light oil if they are naked, none if they are already sauced, preheat so the skin seizes. Salmon: cold or gentle start, brush of oil on the flesh, not a bath. Vegetables: oil is the difference between roasted and dehydrated; still do not fill the drawer to the lip.

Reheat is where preheat earns its keep. A cold leftover dumpling in a cold drawer steams and then toughens. A hot drawer recrisps. Recrisp modes on Crispi and some Ninja programs are just “hot air, short time.” Use them. Do not run a 20-minute roast cycle on last night’s rice. Rice wants a sprinkle of water and a covered microwave more often than it wants a fan.

If you remember only one prohibition, remember the aerosol. If you remember only one permission, remember the cold start for fatty food. If you remember only one habit, remember space. The machine is not a deep fryer with a moral. It is a small oven. Treat oil like you would in a 400°F oven: enough to cook, not enough to paint the walls. The rest is marketing, and marketing does not wash the basket.

A note on “no oil” bags and dry rubs. Spice that is only powder will blow onto the heater and burn. A teaspoon of oil is the glue. Water is not the glue; it steams and then you have paste. Egg white can bind crumbs in a drawer if you insist on breading; it is messier to wash than oil. Choose your mess. August corn on the cob in a Dual Zone wants a brush of oil and a turn, not a spray from a can. Fruit in an air fryer is a novelty and a sugar burn on the plate. Save the drawer for the jobs that benefit from fan and metal, and save the aerosol for the grilling of a backyard you may not have. The basket will last longer than the can’s promise.
