Dual drawer, stack, Crispi, and the Foodi ovens

Side-by-side drawers, stacked baskets, Crispi, and a seventy-dollar oven gap.

August 12, 2026 · Chicago

Ninja sells one idea — hot air, fast — in four cabinets that do not substitute for each other. Side-by-side Dual Zone drawers. Stacked baskets. A glass Crispi pod you can take off the counter. A Foodi oven that sits where a toaster oven sat. The useful work in August 2026 is naming the shape before you name the SKU. People cross-shop a Dual Zone against a DT200 because both are black and both say air fry. They are not the same appliance. One is two mini-ovens with a beep. The other is a countertop oven with a sheet-shaped cavity and a seventy-dollar sibling.

Side-by-side drawers

Dual Zone is the default US recommendation for two people who want a protein and a starch to finish together. DZ201 is 8 quarts; DZ401 is 10. The extra two quarts are height, not a personality. Whole chicken is the test; the Dual Zone note covers that fork. Here the point is the shape: two drawers, wide footprint, low height, you pull like a file cabinet. It lives on a counter and does not pretend to toast a bagel on a rack. If you want two independent times, this is the form. If you want a pizza, it is not.

Drawers punish crowding the same way a basket does. They forgive a weeknight. They do not replace a sheet pan when six people are coming. A family of two is Dual Zone’s home. A family of six that insists on one Ninja cavity is looking at FlexDrawer, which is a wide single drawer with a divider, and is still not an oven. Flex is a sidebar. Do not make it the lead. Dual Zone is the lead for two foods, two drawers, one beep.

Stacked baskets

Double Stack rotates Dual Zone ninety degrees. Two cavities, one above the other, a narrower face, more height. The pitch is a small counter that cannot give up Dual Zone’s width. The cost is a taller object under cabinets, a top basket you cannot see without a squat, and grease that wants to travel. If your constraint is a 16-inch-wide hole between the coffee maker and the wall, stack is the conversation. If your constraint is a 12-inch cabinet lip, stack loses and a low Dual Zone or a Crispi wins.

Stack is not “Dual Zone but better.” It is Dual Zone for a different hole in the kitchen. Capacity numbers look similar in marketing. The geometry of loading does not. You will favor the bottom basket. The top basket becomes leftover fries. That is a human fact, not a review.

Crispi, the pod that leaves

Crispi is a PowerPod that sits on a borosilicate glass container. The 4-quart vessel will take a small chicken; the 6-cup vessel is a personal bowl. The pod comes off. The glass goes in a fridge, a microwave, a dishwasher, a picnic table. That is the entire product. It is not a Dual Zone. It is not an oven. It is the Ninja you can put away, which is the Ninja a small apartment actually uses more than once.

Presets are coarse. There is no Dual Zone pairing. You do one vessel at a time unless you own two pods, which almost nobody does. If the reason you wanted Ninja was two foods at once, Crispi is the wrong aisle. If the reason you wanted Ninja was to stop owning a hulking drawer unit, Crispi is the aisle. It pairs with the Wonder Oven note because both get sold as “the air fryer,” and only one of them is a box that never moves.

The Foodi ovens, and a seventy-dollar gap

DT200 (and the DT201 refresh) is Ninja’s countertop oven that air-fries: a drop-down door, racks, a sheet-shaped cavity, about 1800 watts, a pizza that is actually a pizza. DT500 / DT501 is the Smart XL sibling with a probe and a larger box. US street prices have spent a long time about seventy dollars apart. That gap is the oven fork in this family: do you want a probe and more interior, or do you want the cheaper oven that already replaced your toaster.

The oven shape does work Dual Zone cannot do. Toast. A 12-inch pizza. A tray of cookies that should be a tray. A whole chicken that sits like a chicken, not like luggage in a drawer. Dual Zone does two foods at two temperatures in a footprint the oven will not match. If you already own a wall oven you like, Dual Zone or Crispi is the second tool. If you do not, and the toaster is dying, the DT200-class oven is the tool, and the seventy dollars is whether you want the probe version.

DT200 versus DT500 is not 8 versus 10 quarts. It is a cavity and a thermometer. People who “upgrade” from Dual Zone to DT500 because the listing said XL still have not solved toast. People who skip Dual Zone for a DT200 still have not solved two-drawer weeknights. Buy the shape, then the SKU.

If you are standing in a kitchen trying to pick one Ninja, pick the hole it has to live in. Width, height, whether it can leave, whether toast matters. The SKU table comes after that. The seventy-dollar oven gap is real and boring. The wrong shape is expensive even when it is on sale.

What not to combine

A Dual Zone plus a DT200 is a real kitchen, not a redundancy, if you actually toast and you actually pair a protein with a starch. A Dual Zone plus a Crispi is usually one too many Ninja heat sources and a lid you will not find. A DT500 plus a FlexDrawer is how a counter disappears. Start with one shape. Live with it for a month. The second object, if it arrives, should do the job the first one refused, not the job a listing promised in a different font.

Heat and grease are shared across the family. Drawer liners, oven crumb trays, and Crispi gaskets all fail the same way: people stop cleaning them. Form factor does not rescue a machine that is never emptied. The oven is easier to ignore because it looks like furniture. The Dual Zone announces itself with two baskets in the sink. That announcement is a feature. If you will not wash two drawers, you will not wash a crumb tray either, and you should stay with a sheet pan in the wall oven you already have.

Power is shared too. These are hungry 120-volt appliances. Two Ninja cabinets on one tired kitchen circuit is a breaker conversation, not a recipe conversation. The DT500 does not get a special exemption because it cost seventy dollars more. Plug the oven and the coffee maker on different sides of the kitchen if you can. That is more useful than IQ Boost copy.

August is a quiet month in this aisle. There is no reason to let a holiday graphic pick a shape for you. Dual Zone if two drawers are the point. Stack if width is the constraint and height is free. Crispi if the object has to leave. DT200 if toast and a tray matter; DT500 if the probe and the extra box are worth the seventy dollars. That is the whole map. The SKUs will be renamed again. The holes in the kitchen will not.
