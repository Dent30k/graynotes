Walking pads

Desk height for under-desk walking

Elbows, monitor height, and the two-inch mistake that makes walking at a desk miserable.

August 20, 2026 · Chicago

A walking pad is a two-inch tax on a standing desk. People buy the pad, keep the desk at the height that felt right when they were standing still, and then spend a month wondering why their shoulders live by their ears. The pad is not mysterious. The deck of a W50-class machine is about six inches of belt and frame. Your elbows do not know that. They only know the keyboard got closer to the floor without the chair coming along.

The two-inch mistake

Standing-desk charts assume a flat floor and shoes you will actually wear. Under-desk walking adds the pad’s deck height plus a small bounce. Six inches of deck is not six inches of elbow drop if you walk instead of stand, because walking shortens your effective stature a little: knees bent, torso slightly forward, eyes on a monitor that is now too high relative to a chin that is now too low. People correct the monitor with a stack of books and leave the desk alone. That is the two-inch mistake in the other direction. The fix is to raise the desk until the elbows are near 90 degrees with the shoulders dropped, then drop the monitor to meet the eyes, not the other way around.

A 5'4" walker and a 6'2" walker do not share a height memory on the desk controller. If two people share the desk, program two walking presets and two standing presets. The walking preset is not “standing plus six inches.” It is standing plus deck minus the crouch. On many US desks that works out to about two inches above the still-standing height, not six. Measure. The manufacturer chart is a still photograph.

Elbows, then eyes

Keyboard first. If the desk is a millimeter too low, wrists extend and the pad becomes a story about tendons. If the desk is too high, shoulders hike and the pad becomes a story about a neck. Walking exaggerates both because you cannot rest your weight on the desk without shoving the pad. A slight negative keyboard tilt helps some people; a brick under the back of a keyboard tray helps others. The tray itself is a problem: many trays were designed for a chair, and they bang the pad’s console or the walker’s thighs. If the tray cannot rise with the desk to a walking height, disable it for walking hours.

Monitor second. Eye line on the top third of the screen, neck long, not a laptop on the desktop. A laptop on a walking desk is a hunched 13-inch object at belt height. Arm, riser, or a real monitor. The pad does not create that need; it makes ignoring it impossible. Webcam height follows the monitor. A camera that looked fine when you were still will look up your nose when you walk if you raised the desk and left the laptop closed-loop. This is not vanity. It is why people stop walking on video days, which is most days.

Deck height, shoes, and the chair

W50-class decks sit around six inches. Cheap 40 × 100 cm pads are often lower and less rigid, which feels like a kindness until the belt flexes. Measure the actual pad you own, not the listing. Shoes matter: a 12-millimeter trainer is part of the stack; socks on a belt are a different effective height and a different slip risk. Keep one pair at the desk. Do not mix boots and the walking preset.

The chair has to leave. A rolling task chair behind a pad is a trip if you step back without looking. Park it at 90 degrees, not in the aisle you will use when a call goes badly. Sit-stand-walk is a three-shape day. If the desk cannot drop to a seated typing height after the pad is slid out, you will leave the pad in place and sit with your knees against a motor. That is how decks get scuffed and how knees get a bruise that has nothing to do with fitness.

A working order of operations

Put the pad on the floor, centered, belt parallel to the desk’s front edge. Stand on the locked belt. Raise the desk until elbows are right. Walk at 1.8–2.2 mph, the actual working speed, and recheck elbows because gait changes them. Then set monitor height. Then set webcam. Then save the preset with a name you will recognize at 8 a.m. Repeat in the shoes you will wear in January, not only in August socks. If the desk maxes out before elbows are happy, the pad is too thick for that frame, or you are too tall for that desk’s travel, or both. A 6'4" walker on a desk that only goes to 48 inches with a 6-inch pad is not a willpower problem.

Anti-fatigue mats do not belong on top of a walking belt. They belong beside it, for the hours you stand still. A mat under the pad can reduce thump into the floor below; that is the noise note. It will also raise the stack another half inch. Recheck elbows if you add one. US outlets, US desks, August 2026. The W50 at 4 mph is a different posture than the W50 at 2 mph. Set the desk for the speed you type at, not the speed you brag about.

Monitors, laptops, and the cheap riser

A single 27-inch monitor on an arm is the easy case. Two monitors on a wide bar will torque a cheap desk at walking height and will shake at 2 mph if the pad is not rigid. Tighten the arm. Put the heavier screen on the side you look at, not the side that looks even. A laptop on a riser behind the keyboard is acceptable if the riser is stable and the laptop is not also the only camera. A folding travel riser that flexes is a metronome for your neck.

Lighting goes with eye line. A lamp aimed at a standing face will glare into walking eyes if you raised the desk and left the lamp. Move the lamp or dim it. The pad does not care. Your next four-hour block does.

If you only remember one number, remember this: set height while walking at the speed you work, not while posing on a locked belt. The locked-belt pose is how the two-inch mistake gets into the preset. Unlock, walk, then save. Do it again after a week, because gait settles. The desk will not settle for you.

Children, dogs, and housemates are part of the height problem. A desk at walking height is a chin-level shelf for a child and a tail-level puzzle for a dog. If the room is shared, the walking preset should not be the height you leave the desk at when you go to the kitchen. Drop it. The motor will wait. A lowered desk is also how you keep a cat from sitting on the keyboard you just aligned to a 2 mph gait. This is housekeeping, not a gadget. The two-inch mistake is famous because it is small. The unlowered desk is how the small mistake becomes a household object that other people walk into.
